Your profit and loss says you made $12,000 last month. Your bank balance went down. Both can be right at the same time, and for e-commerce sellers it happens often. Profit and cash measure different things, and the gap between them is where most cash surprises come from.

Why profit and cash are different

Profit counts what you earned and what it cost you to earn it, in the month the sales happened. Cash counts what actually went in and out of your bank. Plenty of money moves through your bank without touching profit, and some profit hasn't reached your bank yet.

For e-commerce sellers, the usual reasons are:

  • Stock you bought but haven't sold. When you pay a supplier, the cash leaves now. In well-kept books, that stock only becomes a cost when it sells. A big stock order can drain your bank in a month that still shows a healthy profit.
  • Supplier deposits. Paying a deposit on next season's order uses cash months before those products earn anything.
  • Money the marketplaces are still holding. Sales from the last days of the month, settlements not yet paid out, and reserves Amazon holds back all count toward profit before the cash reaches you.
  • Loan and financing repayments. The part of a repayment that pays back what you borrowed, including Shopify Capital repayments taken from your payouts, uses cash but isn't a cost in your profit and loss.
  • What you take out of the business. Owner draws reduce your cash but aren't a business expense.

Some things work the other way. Sales tax you've collected but not yet paid sits in your bank, but it isn't yours. Bills you've received but not yet paid have hit profit without leaving your bank. That makes your balance look better than it really is, for now.

An example

Example figures

One month for a seller on Shopify and Amazon:

Profit for the month$12,000
Stock bought, more than the stock that sold−$15,000
Loan repaid (the part that repays the loan)−$2,000
Owner draw−$3,000
More money held by Amazon and Shopify at month end−$1,500
Sales tax collected, not yet paid+$1,500
Change in your bank balance−$8,000

These figures are for illustration only.

Nothing here is a mistake. The business made $12,000 and the bank went down $8,000, and every dollar of the difference has a reason. The problem is only when you can't see those reasons, and find out about the gap when a supplier payment or a tax bill is due.

What fixed looks like

  1. Stock is recorded as something you own when you buy it, and becomes a cost only in the month it sells. That keeps your profit honest in months when you buy heavily.
  2. Money held by Shopify, Amazon and PayPal sits in clearing accounts, so you can see what's yours but not yet in the bank.
  3. Sales tax you've collected is recorded as money you owe, not as cash you can spend.
  4. Every month, you get a cash flow statement next to your profit and loss. It's the report that lines up profit and cash and shows each difference, like the example above.

A quick check you can do today

Take last month's profit from your books and the change in your bank balance over the same month. If the two are far apart and you can't explain the gap in a few lines, ask your bookkeeper for a cash flow statement for that month. If your books can't produce one that makes sense, the stock and clearing accounts behind it probably need work.

How you set aside sales tax, and when it's due, depends on the states you sell into. It's worth confirming with your CPA.
